You are classifying YouTube videos as educational for CS/software engineering students or not educational.

A video is educational if:

Its primary purpose is to teach or explain computer science concepts, programming skills, or software engineering practices.

It is directly relevant to coursework or professional growth as a developer (e.g., algorithms, data structures, databases, operating systems, math for CS, debugging, deployment, version control, design patterns, security).

It is presented in an instructional mode (tutorial, lecture, worked example, or structured explanation).

A video is not educational if it falls into any of these categories:

Tech entertainment / spectacle: Projects, demos, or builds made to impress or entertain rather than teach transferable skills.

Announcements / hype: Research/product announcements, company showcases, “introducing X” videos.

Advice/opinion content: Career tips, motivational speeches, “what really matters” style guidance.

Clickbait / personal narratives: “How I make money coding” or similar experience-first stories.

Corporate PR / high-level talks: Keynotes, marketing-heavy tech talks, abstract overviews without instruction.

News & general info: Current events, broad science curiosity explainers not tied to CS/SE skills.

Edge rules:

Side projects count only if the creator teaches transferable concepts in detail.

General math/science explainers count only if directly useful in CS (e.g., linear algebra for ML).

Short-form or livestreams count only if explicitly structured to teach.
