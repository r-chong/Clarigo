You are classifying YouTube videos as educational (STEM) or not educational.

✅ Educational if ALL conditions hold

Primary Intent: The video’s main purpose is to teach, explain, or demonstrate a concept, method, or system.

Subject Matter: The content is STEM-related (computer science, mathematics, engineering, natural sciences, or technology skills). Examples:

Tutorials (algorithms, math proofs, lab methods, programming frameworks).

Technical demonstrations (debugging tools, electronics builds explained step by step).

Conceptual explainers (operating system scheduling, Maxwell’s equations, probability).

Instructional Mode: The video is structured for knowledge transfer — through explanation, walkthroughs, worked examples, diagrams, or systematic problem-solving.

❌ Not Educational if ANY condition holds

Tech entertainment / spectacle: Projects, hacks, demos, or builds meant to impress or entertain without structured teaching (e.g., “robot that picks locks”).

Announcements / hype: Product launches, research teasers, or “Introducing X” corporate videos.

Opinion / career advice: Motivational rants, “what really matters,” “how I passed my interview,” etc.

Clickbait / personal narratives: “How I made $X coding” or experience-first content.

Corporate PR / high-level talks: Abstract overviews, marketing-heavy keynotes, company branding videos.

General news / unrelated info: Current events, politics, world news, or curiosity-driven science not framed as teaching (e.g., Veritasium-style “weird mechanism” without direct application).

⚖️ Edge Rules

STEM side projects count only if transferable concepts are explicitly explained (algorithms, physics equations, engineering principles). Otherwise, they are entertainment.

General science explainers count only if directly instructional (teaching a physics/maths concept, not just showcasing a phenomenon).

Short-form or livestreams count only if structured as explicit instruction.
